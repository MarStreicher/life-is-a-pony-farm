Visits are by arrangement. A pony farm is still a home, and the field is calmer when the day is planned.

## Before you come

Write ahead and say when you would like to stop by. We will tell you whether the gate is open that day, and whether there is room for a training session on the farm.

When you have a real address, hours, and a way to reach you, put them here. This paragraph is the spot for the lane name, the parking note, and the email or phone number.

## What to expect

Training at the pony farm is simple, patient work. It happens on foot, in the field, and in the rhythm of the day rather than a formal lesson schedule. The goal is not to push a pony into performance, but to build trust, clarity, and confidence in a way that suits the horse as well as the rider.

The pony comes to you when she is ready. You may spend time leading, standing, grooming, and learning how to move without hurry. We work with the horse’s comfort first: quiet handling, soft language, and a pace that allows trust to grow.

There is no arena schedule and no gift shop. Wear shoes you can stand in, leave the dog in the car unless we have said otherwise, and let the pony come to you.

If you bring a treat, ask first. The best thing to bring is time.
